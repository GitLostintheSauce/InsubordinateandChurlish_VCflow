# Where the Money Went: VC Funding, 2022–2025

*A one-page brief. Every figure traces to `sources.md`.*

Between 2022 and 2025, venture capital didn't just shrink and recover — it **reallocated**. The dominant fact of the period is that one sector, artificial intelligence, went from being a large category among several to being *the market itself*. AI startups raised about **$45.8 billion in 2022, then ~$50B, $114B, and finally $211B in 2025** — by which point AI accounted for **roughly half of all global venture dollars**. Nothing else in the data comes close to this trajectory. For an investor, the uncomfortable implication is that "venture capital" in 2025 is largely a bet on a single theme; for a founder outside it, the question is whether you're raising in AI's shadow or competing for the half of the market that's left.

The second story is **concentration**, and it cuts across every sector. The 2021 era of many mid-sized rounds gave way to a handful of enormous ones. OpenAI's **$40B round in early 2025 alone was ~19% of all AI funding that year**, and roughly **15 companies raising $2B+ accounted for ~47% of AI's total**. The same pattern shows up elsewhere: Stripe's $6.5B was ~15% of all 2023 fintech funding; a single Binance-linked $2B deal was ~41% of crypto's Q1 2025. The headline totals are real, but they increasingly describe a small number of winners, not a broad market. Any "sector is back" claim has to be checked against how much of it is one mega-round.

The third story is the **crypto cycle** — the one most relevant to a perpetual-exchange business. Crypto/Web3 funding peaked early in 2022 (Galaxy's quarterly data puts Q1 2022 at **$14.2B**), then collapsed through the FTX-and-rate-shock winter to a trough near **$1.9B per quarter in 2023**, ground sideways in 2024, and **recovered through 2025 to $8.5B in Q4 — the strongest quarter since Q2 2022**. The shape matters more than any single number: capital left, the weak projects died, and the money that returned in 2025 skewed toward infrastructure, exchanges, and trading rails rather than the consumer/NFT froth of 2022. That's a healthier base to build trading infrastructure on than the 2022 peak was.

The fourth shift is quieter but directional: **money rotated from consumer-and-growth toward "hard" categories**. Fintech, the 2021–22 darling, **halved from ~$90B (2022) to ~$43B (2023)** before a selective rebound to ~$52B in 2025 — fewer, larger, more disciplined rounds. Meanwhile defense tech, tiny by comparison (~$2.6–3B/yr in Crunchbase's narrow cut), became a *serious* destination, anchored by Anduril scaling from a $1.48B round in 2022 to **$2.5B in 2025**. The throughline: investors traded growth-at-any-cost consumer plays for AI, defense, and infrastructure — assets with defensible moats or government demand.

**What an investor should take away:** the 2022→2025 move was a flight to AI and to concentration. Crypto is mid-recovery off a real bottom, not at a new peak — which is the right framing for anyone building exchange infrastructure now. **What I'd be cautious claiming:** the Climate and Biotech lines in this dataset shift definitions across years (equity-only vs all-stages; US-only vs global), so their year-over-year *trends* are indicative, not precise — I'd present them with that caveat rather than draw a confident slope. The AI, Fintech, and Web3 stories, by contrast, are well-sourced and defensible.
