🤖 AI — the sector that ate venture

The defining shift of 2022–2025, and where its appetite is now pulling capital next.

AI's takeover of VC

No sector reshaped venture capital like AI. Funding climbed from $45.8B in 2022 to $211B in 2025 — by which point AI was roughly half of all global venture dollars. And it concentrated: per CB Insights' Venture Trends 2025, about 49% of AI funding went to just six companies, and a single OpenAI round (~$40B) was ~19% of the year's AI total. (Charts 1 & 2 above show the full trajectory and the swelling share of the pie.)

The AI–energy nexus

The question I asked: one of those six top-funded AI companies is Aligned, which builds AI data centers — and data centers are enormously power-hungry. So: is venture money following AI's power demand into energy companies? Is it correlated, and is there a gap between startups raising VC and larger players capturing the biggest share? How did it shift from 2024 to 2025?

The answer — yes, and it surged. Disclosed VC funding to energy startups powering AI jumped ~14×, from ~$264M (2024) to ~$3.84B (2025), concentrated in firm, clean baseload — nuclear (X-energy, TerraPower) and fusion (Commonwealth Fusion, Helion) — because data centers need 24/7 power, not intermittent solar/wind. But the larger channel is invisible on this chart: hyperscalers (Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Meta) signed gigawatts of power deals — e.g. Amazon–Talen 1.92 GW and Meta–Constellation 1.12 GW in 2025 — whose dollar values are undisclosed. So venture funds the speculative future supply (measurable, concentrated in a few names — mirroring AI itself); incumbents lock in the real near-term supply (gigawatts, unmeasurable in dollars). The correlation with AI is directional, not proven — two years of data can't establish causation, and electrification/grid trends are also at play.

Process & tools. The question was sparked by CB Insights' Venture Trends 2025 (48% of VC to AI; 49% of AI funding to six companies). The energy data was gathered with ChatGPT Pro in Deep Research mode — chosen over a plain chat model because it returns cited, multi-source results (critical for a no-hallucinated-numbers rule). It was then structured, classified by capital type (VC equity vs. project finance vs. PPA), and visualized in Claude Code.
